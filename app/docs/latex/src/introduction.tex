\section{Introduction}
The digital era has necessitated a re-evaluation of how sacred texts are engaged. Computational Hermeneutics, as defined by Kommers et al. \cite{kommers2026}, provides a framework where AI acts as a "context machine," navigating the inherent situatedness and plurality of ancient texts. Unlike standard search engines, this framework acknowledges the ambiguity of biblical narratives and promotes iterative inquiry.

Furthermore, the Relational Hermeneutic approach emphasized in classical theology \cite{packer1973} suggests that scripture reading is fundamentally a dialogical process. Interpretation is not merely a cognitive exercise but a means of deepening the relationship between the reader and the Divine. The "Hermeneutic of the Child" \cite{nih2024} further advocates for a natural, trust-based interaction that avoids the mechanical rigidity often found in adult-centric study methods \cite{vygotsky1978}.
